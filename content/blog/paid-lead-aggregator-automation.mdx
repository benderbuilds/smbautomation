## How the Shared-Lead Model Actually Works

Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor, and Bark.com all operate on a variation of the same model: a homeowner submits a service request, the platform matches them to contractors in their area who purchased that category, and the same request gets distributed to **3–5 contractors simultaneously**. On Thumbtack, the homeowner request typically goes to 4–5 contractors in the first four hours. On Angi, most leads are shared by default unless you've paid for an exclusive lead package.

What this means in practice: every lead you receive has a countdown timer attached. The moment that notification hits your inbox, your four competitors received the same notification. The homeowner is about to hear from someone. Whether that someone is you depends entirely on how fast your response fires.

This is a structurally different problem than a cold lead or a form submission from your website. With your own inbound leads, you have a reasonable window — often 15–30 minutes before the prospect moves on. With aggregator leads, you're in a race that starts the second the platform sends the notification. **The average contractor on these platforms takes 4–9 hours to respond.** If you're one of them, you're funding the contractors who built their systems to respond in under a minute.

The other cost that doesn't show up in your invoice: **contact rate drops sharply after the first few minutes.** Sources across the industry consistently put the drop between the 5-minute window and the 30-minute window at 3–5x in conversion rate. By the time you're checking leads two hours later, most of those homeowners have already booked, or at minimum have already committed to a conversation with someone else.

## The Response Window That Decides Every Shared Lead

The first principle of making paid aggregator leads work is accepting that the entire game is won or lost in the first five minutes. Not the first appointment. Not the follow-up call. The first response.

That response doesn't need to be complicated. It needs to be:

- **Fast** — under 60 seconds from lead receipt to your message landing on their phone
- **Personal enough** — not obviously a form letter, ideally including their name and the job type they requested
- **Low-friction** — gives them a simple next step (reply, tap a link, schedule a call)

What it doesn't need to do: sell them, explain your company history, or give them a price. The goal of the first response is to open a conversation before your competitors do. Everything else happens after that.

Here's what that message looks like for an HVAC company:

*"Hi [Name] — this is Apex Heating & Cooling. Got your request about [service type]. We're available today and can usually get out same-day for diagnostics. What's the issue you're dealing with? — Mike"*

That message, automated and fired within 60 seconds of a new Angi lead arriving, does three things: it establishes first contact, it signals same-day availability (which matters enormously on service calls), and it invites a reply that starts the booking conversation. The homeowner is still on their phone when it arrives. Your competitors' messages show up 20 minutes later.

## The Automation System That Gets You Under 60 Seconds

The automation that makes sub-60-second lead response work on aggregator platforms runs in two parts: lead intake and instant response.

**Lead intake: getting the notification into your automation system immediately.** Most platforms support lead delivery via email or webhook. The fastest integrations connect via webhook or Zapier, pulling new leads from Angi/Thumbtack/HomeAdvisor directly into your CRM — GoHighLevel, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro — without an email delay. Email-based notifications introduce 1–3 minutes of lag before the trigger fires, which matters when the race is measured in seconds. If your platform offers webhook delivery, use it.

**Instant response: the message sequence that fires on lead receipt.** Here's the full automated flow:

1. **New lead arrives** via webhook or email trigger.
2. **CRM creates a contact record** automatically — name, number, address, job type, platform source.
3. **SMS fires within 30–60 seconds** to the lead's number with a personalized first message.
4. **Email fires simultaneously** as a secondary channel — not all homeowners read texts immediately, but 20–30% of paid lead responses happen via email.
5. **If no reply in 15 minutes**, a second SMS fires: *"Still available today if you need [service type] — just reply here or call us at [number]."*
6. **Lead gets assigned** to the appropriate team member in the CRM for follow-up if no reply after 30 minutes.
7. **If no contact in 2 hours**, an automated call attempt fires — either routed to a live team member or handled by an AI voice agent that can qualify the lead and book the appointment directly.

That last step matters more than most contractors realize. **The [AI receptionist system](/blog/ai-receptionist-local-service-business) doesn't just handle inbound calls — it can be configured to execute outbound call attempts on new aggregator leads when the SMS sequence doesn't get a reply.** An AI agent that calls the lead at the 2-hour mark, introduces itself as your business, confirms the job request, and asks for availability is doing the follow-up that most manual operations skip entirely.

## What Happens After the First Message

Winning the first response gets you the conversation. Closing the job requires the right follow-up for leads who don't reply immediately — and there are more of these than you might expect.

A meaningful percentage of aggregator leads who receive your first message won't reply right away even when they saw it. They're at work, they're comparing prices, they haven't decided yet. The contractors who convert these leads are the ones with a structured follow-up sequence that keeps the door open for 72 hours without feeling like harassment.

**The 3-day follow-up sequence for aggregator leads:**

- **Day 0, Hour 0:** First SMS (automated, fires on lead receipt)
- **Day 0, Hour 2:** Outbound call attempt (manual or AI agent)
- **Day 1, 9 AM:** Follow-up SMS: *"[Name] — just following up on your [service] request. We have availability this week. Would [date] work for you? Takes two minutes to book: [link]"*
- **Day 2, 10 AM:** Email follow-up with more detail — your credentials, what the job typically involves, what to expect on the visit
- **Day 3, 4 PM:** Final SMS: *"Last reach-out on this — if you're still looking for a [service] company, we'd love to help. Here's the easiest way to book: [link]. Otherwise no worries either way."*

This sequence runs automatically, stops the moment the contact replies or books, and handles the contacts who weren't ready on Day 0 without requiring anyone on your team to remember to follow up. ## Narrowing Your Lead Settings to Raise Quality

Speed fixes the response problem. Lead quality settings fix the volume-versus-profit problem.

Most contractors running aggregator platforms buy too broad a category and service area, then wonder why their close rates are low. The fix is to match your settings to the jobs where you actually make money — not the broadest possible net.

Three settings worth reviewing on every aggregator account:

**Job type filtering.** On Angi and Thumbtack, you can filter the specific services you want to receive leads for. HVAC companies buying general "home improvement" categories will receive inquiries for work they don't do — wasted spend. HVAC companies specifying "AC installation," "furnace replacement," and "heat pump installation" only receive leads for high-ticket jobs where their average ticket justifies a $75 lead cost. Narrower categories, higher-quality leads, better close rates.

**Service area radius.** A 50-mile radius generates more leads than a 15-mile radius — and also more leads in markets where you have no brand recognition, can't respond to emergencies quickly, and are competing against companies with home-field advantage. Tightening your radius typically reduces volume by 30–50% but can double your close rate in the reduced geography where you operate at full strength.

**Budget pacing.** Most platforms allow daily or weekly lead caps. Uncapped budgets push more leads than your team can respond to quickly — which defeats the entire first-responder strategy. Cap your daily lead volume to match what your automation can handle with sub-60-second response. Ten leads a day that you respond to in 30 seconds convert better than 40 leads a day that you respond to in 2 hours.

## What to Track (Cost Per Booked Job, Not Cost Per Lead)

The number most contractors track — cost per lead — is the least useful metric for evaluating aggregator spend. A $40 lead that closes is cheaper than a $20 lead that doesn't, and tracking cost per lead without close rate tells you nothing about whether the spend is working.

**Five numbers that actually tell you how your aggregator budget is performing:**

1. **Response time by platform** — the average time between lead receipt and your first contact. Target: **under 2 minutes**. Track this weekly. If it's drifting above 5 minutes, something in the automation is broken or leads are being manually processed.

2. **Contact rate** — the percentage of paid leads you actually reach (they reply, pick up, or confirm an appointment). With fast automated response, target: **40–60%**. Industry average without automation: 20–30%.

3. **Lead-to-booked rate** — the percentage of paid leads that become confirmed appointments. Target: **15–25%** with a working response and follow-up system. Industry average without automation: **10–15%**.

4. **Cost per booked job** — total monthly spend on the platform divided by jobs booked from that source. This is the number that tells you whether a platform is worth running. At a 15% booking rate and $65 average lead cost, your cost per booked job is **$433**. At a 10% rate, it's **$650**. Track this monthly per platform, and cut spend on any source where cost per booked job exceeds your average gross profit per job.

5. **Revenue per booked lead dollar** — total revenue generated from booked aggregator jobs divided by total aggregator spend. For businesses running a healthy aggregator stack, this ratio typically runs **4:1 to 8:1**. Below 3:1 means the channel is losing money or is barely breaking even once you account for overhead.

Run these five metrics on a simple spreadsheet — platform, monthly spend, leads received, contacts made, appointments booked, jobs completed, revenue. That dashboard tells you exactly which platforms are worth scaling, which should be paused, and what your automation improvement is worth in dollars.
