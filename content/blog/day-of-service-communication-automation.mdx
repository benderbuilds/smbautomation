## Two Problems, One System

Before getting into the sequence, it's worth separating these two problems cleanly — because they're often treated as minor irritants rather than what they actually are: recurring, preventable costs.

**The status call problem.** When a customer books a service appointment, they're told an arrival window: "We'll be there between 10 and 2." That window is a promise you made without the ability to know exactly when you'll fulfill it. As the window gets longer — and as your schedule runs late because an earlier job took longer than expected — anxiety builds. The customer is stuck home waiting. By 1:30, they call. This is not a customer service problem. It's an information gap problem. Give them what they need to know before they reach for the phone, and they won't call.

**The failed-access problem.** A technician drives 30 minutes to a job site, knocks, and nobody answers. Maybe the customer forgot the appointment. Maybe they stepped out for 20 minutes. The tech waits, calls the number on file, leaves a voicemail, and drives back. That truck roll cost you $150 and your tech's morning. A day-before reminder and a 30-minute heads-up eliminates the vast majority of these.

## The Five-Touch Day-of-Service Sequence

The sequence has five automated touchpoints — none of them requiring dispatcher action after the initial setup. Here's what fires when, and why each step matters.

**Touch 1 — Evening before: Appointment confirmation + access reminder**

The night before the job (5:00–7:00 PM), an automated text goes to the customer:

> *"Hi [Name] — confirming your [Business Name] appointment tomorrow, [Day]. Your arrival window is [Time] to [Time]. Please make sure someone 18+ is home and that [specific access notes] are ready. Reply CONFIRM to hold your spot or call us if you need to reschedule."*

This is the highest-impact touch in the sequence. Research from Jobber's platform data found that evening-before reminders reduce failed-access rates by **22%** on their own. The message does three things: it reminds the customer the appointment is coming, sets access expectations, and gives them an easy way to confirm or flag a problem before your truck is already on the road.

Include a reply option. If they reply "RESCHEDULE" or don't respond by 8 PM, your dispatcher sees the flag in the morning before dispatch — not after the technician is already there.

**Touch 2 — Morning of: Schedule window update**

At 7:30–8:00 AM on the day of service, a second text fires:

> *"Good morning [Name] — your [Business Name] tech is on the schedule today. Your arrival window is still [Time] to [Time]. We'll text you when they're on the way."*

This handles the customer who woke up unsure the appointment was still on. It also sets the expectation that another message is coming — which dramatically reduces the mid-window anxiety calls.

**Touch 3 — En route: ETA notification**

This is the most critical operational trigger. When the technician marks themselves as "dispatched" or "en route" in your field service software, an automated text fires immediately:

> *"[Tech name] is on the way! Estimated arrival: [Time]. Track their progress: [link] — [Business Name]"*

If your platform supports live GPS tracking links (ServiceTitan, HouseCall Pro, and others do), include it. If not, the ETA alone cuts **40–60%** of inbound status calls on its own. The customer now has what they needed to know. They're not calling anymore.

**Touch 4 — On-site: Arrival notification**

When the technician triggers the "arrived" status in the app, a short text fires:

> *"[Tech name] has arrived. If you have any questions during the visit, [phone number]."*

Its job is acknowledgment. It also closes the loop on the ETA promise — the customer knows the tech is there, so if they were briefly unavailable (grabbing something from outside, on a call) they come to the door instead of wondering if the tech gave up.

**Touch 5 — Job complete: Invoice + review request**

The highest-leverage close in the sequence. The moment the technician marks the job as complete:

> *"Thanks for letting us take care of that today! Your invoice is here: [link]. If we did a good job, a quick Google review means a lot to us: [review link] — [Business Name]"*

Two things happen in one message: payment initiation and review request. The payment link fires while the customer is still in a satisfied post-service state — the best possible moment to collect. And the review request arrives at the highest point of goodwill in the entire customer relationship. Businesses using this sequence report **review conversion rates of 15–25%** on the Touch 5 request, compared to **3–5%** for requests sent days later when the moment has passed.

## What to Track

Five numbers tell you whether this system is performing.

1. **Inbound status calls per day** — Baseline this before going live. After full implementation, target a **40–65% reduction**. If the drop is under 20%, the en-route notification (Touch 3) isn't reaching customers fast enough — usually a lag between when the dispatcher marks a job dispatched and when the SMS fires.

2. **Failed-access rate** — Visits where the technician arrived and no one was home, divided by total jobs run. Target **under 2%**. Industry baseline without day-before confirmation is 6–8%. The evening-before text (Touch 1) with a CONFIRM reply requirement is what drives this metric down.

3. **Average days to invoice payment** — Measured from job-complete timestamp to payment received. With Touch 5 firing via SMS, target is **1–2 days**. If this metric isn't moving, the invoice link in the message is routing through too many steps before the customer can pay.

4. **Review conversion rate** — Reviews generated divided by Touch 5 messages sent. Target **15–25%** at peak post-job timing. Below 10% usually means the review link goes to a friction-heavy page (login required, long form). It should go directly to the Google review prompt, one tap from the text.

5. **Confirmation reply rate** — Percentage of Touch 1 confirmation texts that receive a CONFIRM reply. Target above 50%. If it's below that, either numbers on file are wrong or the CONFIRM instruction is unclear. A reply rate above 70% means your no-access rate will be predictably low before anyone rolls a truck.
