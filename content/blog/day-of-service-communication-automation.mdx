**78% of homeowners say being kept informed about their appointment status is their single top service expectation** — ahead of price, speed, and even quality of work. Yet the average field service business sends exactly one communication after booking: nothing until the tech knocks on the door.

That gap is why your office handles 15 to 25 calls a day from customers asking where their tech is. At 4 minutes per call, that's **more than 100 hours of staff time per year** spent answering questions that a $0.02 text message prevents. And it's why your Google reviews plateau around 3.8 or 4.1 stars — not because your work is mediocre, but because the experience surrounding the work feels disorganized.

Day-of-service communication is the automation layer that runs from the morning of the appointment through the review request. It costs almost nothing to operate, requires no ongoing staff involvement, and solves two problems at once: reducing inbound status calls and capturing reviews from customers who are still warm from a job well done.

This post covers the 5-step sequence, the tools that run it, and the metrics that tell you whether it's working.

## What Day-of-Service Communication Actually Is

Your appointment reminder sequence — the 24-hour and 2-hour confirmations — stops before the job day starts. That's where most businesses think the communication is done. It isn't.

On the day of service, a customer is doing one thing your reminder sequence can't address: waiting. They've cleared their morning. They've arranged to be home. They have no idea if your tech is 10 minutes out or 3 hours away. And unless someone tells them, the only way to get that information is to call your office.

Day-of-service communication is a five-touch automated sequence that answers every question customers have on the day of their appointment — before they think to ask. Each touch fires automatically based on technician location, job status, and completion triggers. No one on your team sends any of them manually.

Here's what the full sequence looks like in practice:

1. **Morning confirmation** (7:00–8:00 AM): Same-day reconfirmation with appointment window
2. **On-my-way notification** (30–45 minutes before arrival): Tech name, photo, and arrival window
3. **ETA update** (10–15 minutes before arrival): Precise window with live tracking link
4. **Job complete notification** (immediately after job close): Summary, invoice link, payment link
5. **Review request** (30–90 minutes after job close): Single-tap Google review link

Each touch is automated. Each one reduces a category of call your staff would otherwise field. Together, they create an experience that feels managed and professional — the kind that earns 5-star reviews not because every job goes perfectly, but because the customer never felt uninformed.

## Touch 1: Morning Confirmation

Your appointment reminder sequence confirmed the job 24 hours and 2 hours before. The morning confirmation does something different: it shows up at **7:00–8:00 AM on the day of the appointment** to tell the customer they're still on the schedule and when to expect service.

A significant portion of same-day cancellations and missed appointments happen because something came up between the day-before reminder and the morning of the job. The customer forgot to clear their schedule, didn't realize the window was this morning, or had a change of plans they meant to communicate but didn't. A morning confirmation catches all of these at the lowest-cost point — before your tech has left for the job.

The message is simple:

*"Good morning [Name] — your [service type] appointment with [Company] is today between [time window]. Your technician will text you 30 minutes before they head your way. Any questions? Reply here or call [number]."*

This single message does three things: it reconfirms the appointment exists, it sets the expectation for what's coming next, and it creates a natural off-ramp for customers who need to reschedule before anyone drives anywhere.

The [appointment reminder automation post](/blog/appointment-reminder-automation) covers the 24-hour and 2-hour confirmation sequence that runs before this. The morning touch is what bridges that sequence to the on-my-way notification and closes the communication gap between "confirmed yesterday" and "arriving today."

## Touch 2: On-My-Way Notification With Tech Bio

This is the highest-value single message in the entire sequence.

The on-my-way notification fires when your technician is **30–45 minutes from the job address**. It includes three things:
- The technician's first name and photo
- The arrival window (e.g., "between 10:15 and 10:45")
- An optional link to real-time GPS tracking

The photo is not a nice-to-have. **73% of homeowners report feeling more comfortable answering the door for a service technician they can identify in advance.** Including a headshot of the tech who's coming eliminates the "who is this person" anxiety that makes customers either call to confirm or simply not answer the door.

A practical example of what this message looks like:

*"Mike is on his way and will arrive between 10:20 and 10:40. [Photo] You can track his location here: [link]. See you shortly! — Apex Plumbing"*

This message eliminates the most common version of the "where is my tech?" call — the one that comes in 30 minutes after the appointment window starts because the customer hasn't heard anything. Once customers know the tech is en route and have a face to expect, **inbound status calls drop by an estimated 40–60%** compared to businesses that send nothing until arrival (based on industry vendor data; your own result will vary by call volume and existing practices).

The failed-access problem is also worth flagging here. **Field service businesses lose an average of 2–4 jobs per week to no-access situations** — the tech arrives, nobody answers the door, and the appointment is wasted. Failed access is distinct from a no-show: the customer was home, but didn't hear the knock, didn't recognize the truck, or wasn't ready. The on-my-way notification with photo solves this. A customer who just received a text showing Mike's face and a 20-minute arrival window is waiting for Mike. They answer the door.

## Touch 3: Real-Time ETA Updates

When your tech is **10–15 minutes from the address**, a second message fires with a tighter arrival window or live GPS link. This is especially important for service areas with traffic variability or wide initial arrival windows (a 4-hour window vs. a 1-hour window).

For businesses using GPS tracking in platforms like ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Workiz, this touch can include a real-time map link:

*"Mike is 12 minutes away and on track for your 10:30 arrival. Track his route here: [link]."*

For businesses not yet on GPS-integrated platforms, the 10-minute notification with a confirmed narrow window achieves almost the same result. The customer knows to expect someone immediately — and your office doesn't get a call asking "is he still coming?"

**ServicePower's 2024 field service data put arrival window accuracy at 92%** for companies using automated ETA notifications versus 65% for manual dispatch communication. That 27-point accuracy gap directly translates to customer satisfaction: customers who receive an ETA that matches actual arrival give **35% higher satisfaction ratings** on post-job surveys than those who receive an ETA that misses by more than 30 minutes.

The principle is simple: it's not the wait that frustrates customers. It's the uncertainty. Customers who know a tech is 12 minutes away wait calmly. Customers who were told "sometime between 10 and 2" and are now at 1:45 PM with no update are the ones calling your office and leaving frustrated Google reviews.

## Touch 4: Job Complete Notification and Instant Invoice

The moment your technician marks the job complete in your field service platform, two automations fire:

1. **Job complete notification** — a text to the customer confirming the work is done, with a link to the job summary (photos, notes, work completed) and a digital invoice
2. **Payment link** — if payment wasn't collected on-site, the digital invoice includes a one-tap payment option

This touch does more than close the communication loop. It speeds up your collections cycle. **Invoices delivered digitally and immediately after job completion get paid 60% faster** than invoices sent end-of-day or next-day batch. For businesses doing 150+ service calls per month, that acceleration compounds into real working capital improvement — less money sitting in aging receivables, more available for payroll and materials.

The job summary link — especially if it includes before/after photos from the tech's mobile app — also serves a secondary purpose: it gives you documentation if there's ever a dispute, and it gives the customer a clear record of what was done, which reduces callbacks asking "what did you actually fix?"

## Touch 5: The Review Request in the Golden Window

The review request is the last touch, and the timing is what makes it work.

The research is consistent: **review requests sent within 30–90 minutes of job completion convert at 3x the rate** of requests sent the following day. That window is your customer's peak goodwill moment — the job is done, the problem is solved, and they haven't yet moved on with their day. Ask them to share that experience while it's still fresh.

The message should be short and specific:

*"Hi [Name], thanks for choosing Apex — hope Mike took great care of you today. If you have a moment, a quick Google review means a lot: [direct review link]. Takes about 30 seconds. — The Apex Team"*

Three things make this work:
- **The direct link** goes straight to the Google review form, not your profile page. Every extra click reduces completion rates.
- **The timing** hits while the customer is still in the positive frame from a completed job.
- **The personal reference** (Mike, not "your technician") reinforces the human experience the customer just had.

Businesses running this review request as part of a post-job automation sequence consistently generate **4–6x more monthly Google reviews** than businesses asking for reviews manually or ad hoc. At **35 new reviews per month versus 7**, the compounding SEO effect is significant: Google Local searches rank businesses with higher review velocity, and a business moving from 47 to 180 reviews over 12 months gains measurable organic visibility without changing anything else about their marketing.

## What This Sequence Looks Like in Practice

Here's the full day-of-service sequence for a Tuesday morning plumbing job, from the customer's perspective:

- **7:15 AM:** SMS — "Good morning Sarah — your plumbing appointment with Apex is today between 9:00 and 11:00 AM. Your tech will text when they're 30 min out."
- **9:22 AM:** SMS — "Mike is on his way and will arrive between 9:50 and 10:05. [Photo of Mike] You can track his location here: [link]."
- **9:48 AM:** SMS — "Mike is about 12 minutes away — see you soon!"
- **10:42 AM:** (Mike completes the job and marks it done in Housecall Pro) — Automated SMS fires: "Thanks Sarah — your job's complete! View your service summary and invoice here: [link]. Any questions, call us at [number]."
- **11:15 AM:** SMS — "Hi Sarah, hope Mike took great care of you today. If you have a second, a quick Google review means a lot: [link]. Thanks!"

Sarah didn't call the office once. She knew exactly what was happening at every stage. Mike showed up, did the work, and 30 minutes later Sarah's tapping through to leave a 5-star review because the experience felt organized and professional from start to finish.

Now multiply that by 180 jobs per month.

## What to Track

Five numbers tell you whether the day-of-service sequence is working:

**1. Inbound "status check" call volume** — track how many calls come in during working hours from customers asking about appointment status, ETA, or whether the tech is still coming. This number should drop 30–50% within the first 30 days of running the full sequence.

**2. Failed access rate** — track the percentage of jobs where the tech arrived but couldn't access the property (customer not home, not ready, didn't answer). Target: under 3%. Most businesses baseline at 5–10% before running any on-the-way communication.

**3. Same-day cancellation rate** — how many booked jobs cancel the morning of? The morning confirmation catches a portion of these before the tech is dispatched. Target: under 4%.

**4. Review request conversion rate** — of the total post-job review requests sent, what percentage result in a completed Google review? Target: **15–25%** for SMS requests. Below 10% usually means the timing is off or the review link isn't going directly to the review form.

**5. Monthly review volume** — total new Google reviews per month, tracked against the 12 weeks before implementing the sequence. This is the lagging indicator that shows you whether the system is building your local SEO position over time.

Most businesses implementing this sequence for the first time find the inbound call reduction and review velocity improvement within the first 60 days. The failed-access drop typically follows within the first two weeks once the on-my-way notification with tech photo is live.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What percentage of inbound calls to a service business are "where is my tech?" status questions?**
Industry estimates put status-check calls at 20–35% of daily inbound call volume for active service businesses. For a shop taking 60 inbound calls per day, that's 12–21 calls averaging 4 minutes each — 48–84 minutes of staff time per day answering questions that automated notifications eliminate.

**Q: Do customers actually open and read same-day service notifications?**
SMS messages — the primary channel for day-of-service notifications — have an average open rate of 98%, with 90% read within 3 minutes of delivery. The on-my-way notification in particular has near-universal open rates because customers are actively waiting for it.

**Q: How does tech photo inclusion in the on-my-way notification affect results?**
Businesses including technician photos in on-my-way notifications report measurably lower failed-access rates compared to text-only messages. The photo gives customers a face to expect and eliminates uncertainty about whether the person at the door is the right person, which reduces the "I didn't answer because I wasn't sure it was them" access failure.

**Q: How long does this sequence take to set up?**
For businesses on ServiceTitan or Housecall Pro, most of the notifications are already available natively and can be activated and configured in 2–4 hours. The primary setup work is writing message copy, uploading technician photos, and connecting the review request link. For businesses on Jobber + GoHighLevel, full implementation typically runs 4–8 hours, most of which is the GoHighLevel workflow build.
