**The average HVAC company closes 25–32% of its in-home replacement conversations.** The top 10% close **60–70%**. Same market. Same leads. Same pricing.

For a 6-tech shop running 120 service calls per month at an average ticket of $1,100, the difference between a 32% close rate and a 47% close rate is **$19,800 per month in additional revenue** — $237,600 per year — from jobs that were already in the building, with a customer already in the room.

That gap isn't a talent gap. It's a feedback gap. The businesses closing at 60% have a system that tells them exactly what's happening on every call and exactly what to fix. Most businesses have nothing — the tech drives out, closes the job or doesn't, and the manager never knows why.

AI call coaching is the mechanical solution to that mechanical problem. Here's what it is, how it works, and what it costs.

## Why Close Rates Are Lower Than They Should Be

Most service businesses have no visibility into what happens during the in-home conversation. The tech presents options and either closes the job or gets "let me think about it." If they don't close, the manager hears about it as a lost job — not as a specific, correctable moment in the conversation.

The result: the same mistakes repeat on every call. The tech who rushes through options without anchoring the problem first keeps doing it. The one who caves on price the moment a customer pauses keeps doing it. The one who forgets to present financing keeps doing it. Without a transcript, there's nothing to coach.

Traditional fix: role-play training. The manager demonstrates the right approach, runs a few mock calls, and expects behavior to change in the field. It doesn't — not permanently. Training from last Tuesday doesn't stay with a tech on a call in a customer's attic on a Thursday afternoon. It doesn't survive the first difficult customer or the first real objection.

The problem isn't that your techs don't know the right behaviors. It's that there's no feedback loop tying the specific call to the specific mistake. Without that, behavior doesn't change.

AI call coaching closes that loop — at the call level, with specific evidence, every single time.

## What AI Call Coaching Actually Is

AI call coaching is not a training program. It's not a CRM. It's not a script enforcer.

It's a system that records every in-home service call or phone call, transcribes the conversation, and runs the transcript through an AI model that scores the call against proven sales behaviors. The output is a scorecard for each call: which behaviors landed, which were missed, and exactly where in the conversation things went off track.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

A plumbing tech runs a service call for a water heater issue. The call records automatically — the customer hears a brief disclosure at the start, which handles consent. After the tech leaves, the AI processes the recording. By the time the tech is on the highway to the next job, they have a scorecard on their phone showing: (1) they spent 4 minutes on the diagnostic explanation — on target; (2) they presented three options as trained; (3) they did not mention financing before anchoring the price; (4) when the customer said "let me think about it," they accepted the out instead of asking a clarifying question.

That's coaching the manager couldn't deliver — because the manager wasn't there. And it's specific. Not "close better next time" — "offer financing before price, and ask what's holding them back when they go quiet."

Multiply that feedback loop across 20 techs, 400 calls per month, over six months — and you're not running the same team anymore.

## The Three Behaviors AI Scoring Catches Most

The AI doesn't evaluate whether the tech was likable. It scores specific, coachable behaviors. The ones that move close rates most consistently:

**Discovery before diagnosis.** Top closers ask two to three questions about how the problem has been impacting the homeowner before presenting the fix. "How long has this been going on?" "What's your biggest concern — the cost, the timeline, or making sure this doesn't happen again?" Techs who skip this step present solutions before the customer feels heard — and customers who don't feel heard don't buy. AI scoring flags every call where diagnosis came before discovery.

**Presenting financing before price.** ACHR News analysis found contractors who offer financing close at **49% versus 38% for those who don't** — an 11-point difference on the same lead. But financing only helps if the tech brings it up before anchoring the price. A tech who says "$4,800" and then adds "oh, we also have financing" is too late — the sticker shock already hit. AI catches every call where price landed before financing.

**Handling "let me think about it."** This is the highest-leverage moment in any service call and the one most techs handle worst. The default response is acceptance — "Of course, here's my card." The trained response is one clarifying question: "Of course. Is there a part of this you'd like me to walk through again?" That question surfaces the actual objection 40–60% of the time. AI catches every missed opportunity.

The systems consistently lifting close rates aren't coaching on personality. They're drilling these three mechanics, call by call, with specific evidence from the actual conversation.

## Tech Resistance: How to Roll This Out Without Killing Morale

Most owners hesitate because they expect pushback. "They're going to feel like they're being watched." Some will. Here's how to handle it.

**Frame it as evidence of investment, not surveillance.** The teams that adopt coaching fastest understand it this way: the company is investing in making you better, which means earning more. Techs paid on commission internalize this quickly — a 15% improvement in close rate is 15% more money in their pocket. That reframe changes the conversation.

**Share scores publicly but highlight wins first.** Build a brief weekly coaching cadence: 20 minutes, one call reviewed per tech, wins before corrections. Make the scorecard a tool for recognition, not just correction.

**Let your top closers teach.** The most effective coaching isn't the manager explaining the right behavior — it's playing a recording of your best closer handling an objection well and asking the room "what did they do there?" Evidence from real calls in your company, in your market, is more credible than any role-play.

You'll lose one or two techs who refuse to be coached. That's information worth having. The ones who stay and engage are the ones building your business.

## What to Track Once It's Live

Five numbers tell you whether the investment is working:

1. **Close rate by tech** — Track monthly per individual. The goal isn't uniformity; it's trend. A tech moving from 28% to 36% over 90 days is working. One holding flat at 28% despite active coaching is a different conversation.

2. **Average ticket size by tech** — Close rate and ticket size move together when coaching is effective. A tech closing more jobs should also be closing bigger ones. If close rate rises but ticket doesn't, the tech is closing easier jobs — not getting better at the hard ones.

3. **Financing mention rate** — What percentage of calls include a financing offer before price presentation? This should be near 100% within 60 days. It's the fastest-moving metric because it's a behavior toggle, not a skill build.

4. **"Think about it" recovery rate** — Of calls where the customer signals hesitation, what percentage of techs ask a clarifying question? Pull this directly from AI scoring. The industry baseline is around 20%; target 60%+.

5. **Revenue per service call** — Total monthly revenue divided by total service calls run. This is the number that tells you whether coaching ROI is reaching the bottom line. Businesses running consistent coaching cycles see this metric move **15–25% in the first 90 days**.
