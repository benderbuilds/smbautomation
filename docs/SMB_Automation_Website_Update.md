**H2:** Questions business owners ask first.

**What exactly is a Business Efficiency Audit?** A structured review of how work moves through your business. We identify repetitive tasks, unreliable handoffs, missed follow-up, revenue leaks, software gaps, and opportunities to improve the process through automation, AI, or better use of your existing tools.

**How much does it cost?** $1,500, fixed. Implementation is optional and typically ranges from $5,000 to $20,000+ depending on scope.

**Do I need to know what I want to automate?** No. Identifying the right opportunities is the purpose of the audit. Starting with a technology before understanding the problem is one of the most common ways businesses waste money.

**Who is this not for?** Pre-revenue businesses, companies without repeatable workflows yet, anyone looking for general AI training or a list of tools, and buyers shopping purely on price. If your business is not prepared to invest at least $5,000 in implementation, the audit will not pay for itself.

**Will you recommend tools we already use?** Whenever possible. The first question is always whether your existing systems can solve the problem. New software gets recommended only when it provides a meaningful advantage.

**Do you build custom software?** Sometimes, but only when an off-the-shelf option cannot reasonably solve the problem. Most businesses improve significantly by properly configuring and connecting tools they already own.

**Are you tied to specific software companies?** No. Recommendations are based on fit, cost, usability, integrations, security requirements, and expected return.

**Can you work with our existing IT company?** Yes. We provide the roadmap and work alongside internal employees, IT providers, software vendors, agencies, and other specialists.

**Is AI always part of the recommendation?** No. The right answer may be better software configuration, a traditional automation, a redesigned process, employee training, or no change at all. The goal is to improve the business, not force AI into every workflow.

## **2\. Application Form and Auto-Qualification**

This is the most important mechanic on the site. Build it carefully.

#### *Qualification Logic*

An application auto-qualifies when **both** conditions are true:

1. Employees is one of: 5 to 10, 11 to 25, 26 to 50, 51 to 100, More than 100

2. Either revenue is $1 million to $2 million or higher, **or** monthly volume is 400 to 999 or higher

**Qualified path:** redirect to /apply/qualified, which embeds the Calendly inline widget for the 20-minute fit call. Prefill name, email, and company into the Calendly embed. Send a confirmation email with the same booking link as backup.

**Not-qualified path:** redirect to /apply/received. Send an email within one business day. Include the Single Workflow Audit link on both the page and in the email.

Both paths write the full submission to the CRM with the qualification flag, the UTM parameters, and the referring page.

#### *Form Requirements*

* Single page, no multi-step wizard. Target completion under three minutes.

* Work email validation. Reject common free-mail domains with a soft warning, not a hard block.

* Company website is required and must validate as a URL.

* Honeypot field plus Cloudflare Turnstile for spam. Do not use a visible CAPTCHA.

* Preserve form state on validation error. Do not clear the form.

* All submissions are also emailed to jesse@smbautomation.io via Resend with a subject line that includes company name and the qualification result.
