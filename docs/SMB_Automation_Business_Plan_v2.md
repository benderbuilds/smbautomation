# **SMB Automation Business Plan**

Version 2\. Prepared for internal use and dev team handoff.

---

## **1\. Executive Summary**

SMB Automation helps established small and midsize businesses identify where manual processes, disconnected software, and inconsistent follow-up are costing them time and money.

The primary offer is a fixed-fee Business Efficiency Audit at $1,500. The audit examines how work moves through the company, identifies the highest-value opportunities for automation and process improvement, estimates the financial impact, and provides a prioritized implementation roadmap.

Clients receive a practical playbook they can implement internally or use with another provider. SMB Automation also offers implementation and ongoing optimization services.

### **Revenue Model**

* Business Efficiency Audit: $1,500 (primary offer)

* Single Workflow Audit: $495 (downsell and small-business entry point, not promoted on the homepage)

* Implementation projects: typically $5,000 to $20,000+

* Ongoing Optimization Partner: $2,000 to $5,000+ per month

The audit generates immediate revenue, qualifies serious buyers, defines implementation scope, and reduces the amount of unpaid consulting required before proposing larger projects.

### **What Changed in Version 2**

* The free Business Efficiency Score has been removed from the launch scope. It is deferred to Phase 2 and is contingent on a functioning Meta retargeting audience existing first.

* The homepage now carries a single call to action. The $495 Single Workflow Audit has been moved to its own page and referenced only from the FAQ and from workflow-specific blog articles.

* The audit qualification criteria have been revised to stop excluding multi-provider clinics, which are a primary near-term cash-flow vertical.

* Property management is now the lead vertical rather than one of four equally weighted options.

* Proof strategy now leads with operator credibility numbers instead of unrelated marketing metrics.

* A findings guarantee has been added to reduce first-buyer risk.

* A sample audit deliverable is now a launch requirement, not a day 15 to 30 item.

## **2\. The Business Opportunity**

Most established small businesses know they could operate more efficiently, but they struggle to determine:

* Which processes should be automated

* Which tools they actually need

* Whether AI is appropriate for their business

* What an automation project should cost

* How much time or money an improvement could produce

* Which opportunity should be addressed first

Software companies sell tools. Developers build what clients ask for. Generic AI consultants lead with technology.

SMB Automation begins with the business process. The company identifies the operational problem, quantifies the potential impact, and recommends the simplest practical solution.

### **Core Value Proposition**

We find the manual work costing your business time and money, calculate what it is worth to fix, and give you the exact plan and tools to fix it.

## **3\. Ideal Customer Profile**

### **Qualification Criteria**

A prospect qualifies for the full Business Efficiency Audit when they meet the employee threshold and either the revenue threshold or the volume threshold.

* Employees: 5 to 100

* Revenue: $1 million or more in annual revenue, **or** 400 or more customer transactions, appointments, service calls, or units under management per month

* At least one administrative, operations, sales, customer service, or finance function

* Repetitive customer or internal workflows

* Multiple software systems that do not communicate effectively

* A measurable cost associated with slow response, manual work, errors, or missed follow-up

* An owner, CEO, president, COO, or operations leader who can approve a $5,000 to $20,000 project

The volume threshold exists so that high-transaction businesses under $1 million in revenue still qualify. A three-provider med spa running 600 appointments a month has more operational complexity than a $3 million company with two employees and one customer. Volume is the better predictor of audit value than revenue alone.

The strongest qualifying factor is operational complexity, not industry.

### **Poor-Fit Prospects**

* Pre-revenue startups

* Solopreneurs looking for free AI advice

* Businesses without defined processes

* Companies primarily seeking employee training

* People looking for lists of AI tools

* Buyers shopping only for the cheapest developer

* Businesses unwilling to change existing workflows

* Companies unable to invest at least $1,500 in diagnosis or $5,000 in implementation

Prospects who fail qualification but have one clear, defined problem should be routed to the $495 Single Workflow Audit rather than rejected.

## **4\. Industry Focus**

### **Lead Vertical: Property Management**

Property management is the primary vertical. It is where SMB Automation has the deepest and most defensible domain credibility, and where the buyer can be reached through an existing warm network rather than cold acquisition.

Best fit:

* 500 or more units

* 10 or more employees

* Multiple communities or markets

* Centralized leasing, maintenance, accounting, or resident support

Common opportunities:

* Leasing lead response

* Tour scheduling

* Maintenance intake and routing

* Renewal workflows

* Delinquency communication

* Resident onboarding

* Owner reporting

* Review requests

Marketing weight, case study priority, and content production should favor this vertical until it is either proven or disproven.

### **Secondary Vertical: Healthcare and Wellness**

Best fit:

* Multiple providers or locations

* 400 or more appointments per month

* Recurring or membership revenue

* Significant onboarding, scheduling, refill, lab, or follow-up processes

Common opportunities:

* Lead and patient intake

* Appointment reminders

* Patient onboarding

* Lab and refill workflows

* Retention and reactivation

* Internal reporting

* Customer support

Healthcare work must be scoped around appropriate systems, privacy requirements, and compliance obligations. This vertical is prioritized for near-term cash flow because sales cycles are shorter and decision makers are typically the owner.

### **Secondary Vertical: Home and Local Services**

Best fit:

* $2 million or more in annual revenue

* Five or more field employees

* Dedicated office or dispatch staff

* Meaningful inbound lead volume

Common opportunities:

* Missed-call recovery

* Lead response

* Estimate follow-up

* Scheduling and dispatch

* Appointment reminders

* Review generation

* Customer reactivation

* Membership renewals

* Invoice follow-up

### **Opportunistic: Professional Services**

Law firms, accounting firms, insurance agencies, consulting firms, and financial service companies. Common opportunities include client intake, document collection, scheduling, project updates, proposal follow-up, reporting, and recurring client communication.

No dedicated marketing spend for this vertical at launch. Take the work when it comes inbound.

### **Positioning Statement**

## **6\. Offer Structure**

### **Offer 1: Business Efficiency Audit**

**Price:** $1,500 fixed fee. This is the primary offer and the only offer promoted on the homepage.

**Purpose:** An organization-level review of operational processes producing a prioritized roadmap for reducing manual work, improving follow-up, increasing capacity, and recovering lost revenue.

**Includes:**

* Detailed pre-audit questionnaire

* 60- to 90-minute workflow discovery session

* Review of existing software and major recurring processes

* Optional short questionnaires with relevant employees

* Eight to fifteen automation or process opportunities

* Opportunity matrix ranked by impact, cost, complexity, and risk

* Estimated weekly time savings

* Estimated annual labor value

* Estimated revenue opportunities where appropriate

* Recommended software and automation architecture

* Prioritized 90-day implementation roadmap

* Implementation budget ranges

* 60-minute findings presentation

* Complete written playbook owned by the client

* Delivery within seven business days after discovery is complete

**Findings Guarantee:** If the audit does not identify at least $15,000 in annualized time or revenue opportunity, the fee is refunded in full and the client keeps the roadmap. This is a launch-period offer to reduce first-buyer risk while there are no audit case studies. Review after the first ten audits.

**Credit Policy:** The $1,500 fee may be credited toward an implementation project of $7,500 or more started within 30 days. Present this as an incentive, not as evidence the audit lacks standalone value.

**Margin Protection:** Price is held at $1,500 for launch. Because the deliverable is scope-heavy at this price, delivery hours must be tracked on every audit from the first engagement. If the median audit exceeds 12 hours of total delivery time across the first five audits, either the price moves to $2,500 or the deliverable is reduced to five to eight opportunities and a 60-day roadmap. Do not carry an unmeasured scope indefinitely.

**Future Pricing:** After five to ten successful audits and multiple case studies, test $2,000, then $2,500, then higher custom pricing for multi-location or high-complexity companies.

### **Offer 2: Single Workflow Audit**

**Price:** $495 fixed fee.

**Purpose:** For businesses that already know which process is causing problems. Examples: lead follow-up, scheduling, client onboarding, reporting, review requests, invoice collection, maintenance intake, document processing.

**Includes:**

* Structured intake

* Review of one defined workflow

* Current-state process map

* Three to five improvement opportunities

* Estimated time and financial impact

* Recommended tools and automation approach

* Written 30-day implementation plan

* 30-minute findings call

* Delivery within five business days

**Scope Controls:** Limited to one clearly defined workflow. Excludes company-wide review, multiple departments, implementation specifications for unrelated processes, extensive employee interviews, custom software design, and implementation work.

**Credit Policy:** The full $495 may be credited toward a Business Efficiency Audit or implementation project started within 30 days.

**Placement:** This offer does not appear on the homepage. It lives on its own page at /single-workflow-audit and is surfaced through:

* One FAQ answer on the homepage

* Inline CTAs on workflow-specific blog articles

* The disqualification path from the audit application

* Follow-up email to prospects who applied but did not qualify or did not buy

No sales call is required before purchasing. Direct checkout.

### **Offer 3: Implementation**

**Public positioning:** $5,000 to $20,000+ depending on workflows, systems, integrations, users, and departments.

**Internal pricing structure:**

*Quick-Win Implementation:* $3,000 to $5,000. One tightly defined workflow such as missed-call follow-up, review requests, appointment reminders, basic lead intake and routing, or simple internal reporting. Not the primary public anchor.

*Core Implementation:* $7,500 to $15,000. Several connected workflows, CRM implementation or optimization, lead intake and follow-up systems, customer onboarding, scheduling and communication systems, reporting and dashboards, department-level automation.

*Operational Transformation:* $15,000 to $30,000+. Multiple departments, multiple locations, complex integrations, significant data migration, custom internal applications, advanced reporting, high-volume or mission-critical workflows.

**Every implementation includes:** defined baseline, clear success metrics, written scope, workflow design, build and configuration, testing, documentation, team training, handoff or ongoing support, and a 30-day results review.

**Scope management:** Every proposal must document what is included, what is excluded, which systems are involved, client responsibilities, software costs, number of revision rounds, the change-order process, and the support period.

### **Offer 4: Ongoing Optimization Partner**

**Pricing:** $2,000 to $5,000+ per month. This is an ongoing operations and automation partnership, not a software-support plan.

*Optimization Support*, from $2,000 per month: monitoring existing workflows, troubleshooting, minor improvements, monthly performance review, limited consulting hours.

*Growth Optimization*, from $3,500 per month: workflow monitoring, ongoing improvements, one new workflow or significant enhancement per month, performance reporting, software evaluation, priority support.

*Embedded Automation Partner*, from $5,000 per month: ongoing workflow development, cross-department planning, vendor management, reporting and optimization, team training, quarterly efficiency reviews, priority access, strategic operations support.

Avoid retainers below $2,000 per month unless support requirements are extremely limited and clearly defined.

### **Deferred: Free Business Efficiency Score**

A free self-assessment quiz was considered for launch and has been deferred to Phase 2\.

Reasoning: a free automated diagnostic was previously removed from the site because it undercut the paid audit and attracted prospects who could not buy. A quiz is the same product in a different wrapper. It also has no reason to exist without a Meta retargeting audience to feed, and Meta spend does not begin until day 61 at the earliest.

Revisit only when both of the following are true: Meta retargeting is running with a meaningful audience, and the paid audit has proven conversion from at least one other channel.

### **Google Search**

Google is the primary cold paid-acquisition channel at launch because it captures prospects already searching for help.

Initial keyword themes: business automation consultant, workflow automation consultant, AI consultant for small business, business process automation consultant, automate my business, AI implementation consultant, property management automation consultant, home service automation consultant, operations automation consultant, CRM automation consultant.

LinkedIn is the primary social channel. The buyer for all three priority verticals is reachable there, and Facebook targeting is misaligned with B2B decision makers.

Cadence: founder-led posts covering operational problems, audit findings patterns, and vertical-specific observations. Direct outreach to the warm network runs through the same channel.

Meta is retargeting only. Retarget website visitors, blog readers, video viewers, and audit applicants who did not complete.

There is no cold Meta prospecting at launch. Cold Meta traffic has no qualified offer to receive now that the free score is deferred, and B2B targeting on the platform is weak for this buyer.

Creative themes: hidden administrative labor, slow lead response, missed follow-up, employees copying data between systems, software that is not being used properly, owners who remain the bottleneck.

Example hook: your business may not need more employees, it may need fewer manual handoffs.

## **9\. Sales Process**

### **Business Efficiency Audit**

1. Prospect completes a short application, approximately three minutes.

2. Applications that meet the qualification criteria see the scheduling link immediately on the confirmation page. No waiting period.

3. Applications that do not meet the criteria receive a confirmation that a response is coming within one business day, and are routed by email to the Single Workflow Audit.

4. Prospect completes a 20-minute fit call.

5. Prospect purchases the audit.

6. Discovery is completed.

7. Analysis and roadmap are prepared.

8. Findings are presented.

9. Client chooses internal implementation or requests a proposal.

10. Implementation proposal is delivered within two business days.

The auto-qualification rule at step 2 is the single most important conversion mechanic in the funnel. A prospect who is ready to book should never be made to wait a day for manual review.

**Application questions:** company name, company website, applicant role, number of employees, annual revenue range, monthly transaction or unit volume, primary industry, biggest operational bottleneck, current software systems, desired outcome, expected implementation timeline.

### **Single Workflow Audit**

1. Prospect purchases directly through checkout. No sales call.

2. Prospect completes structured intake.

3. SMB Automation reviews the workflow.

4. Findings and action plan are delivered.

5. A 30-minute findings call is completed.

6. Client may proceed to implementation or the full audit.

## **15\. Primary Risks**

### **The audit attracts businesses that cannot afford implementation**

Display the $1,500 price. State the qualification criteria once, clearly. Ask company size, revenue, and volume in the application. Use industry-specific pages. Require a company website and work email. State that implementation typically ranges from $5,000 to $20,000+.

### **The audit takes too long to deliver**

Standardize intake. Limit discovery time. Use reusable scoring and ROI templates. Define the number of workflows reviewed. Restrict employee interviews. Track delivery hours on every engagement. Act on the 12-hour median trigger.

### **Recommendations feel generic**

Use the client’s actual software and workflow volumes. Document the current-state process. Quantify time and financial impact. Name specific tools. Include implementation steps. Explain what should remain human. Identify recommendations that are not worth pursuing.

### **Implementation becomes an unprofitable custom agency**

Maintain a public $5,000 implementation anchor. Build from defined project scopes. Reuse technical components. Use trusted contractors. Separate strategy from build work. Require change orders. Track gross margin by project. Avoid open-ended promises.

### **The Single Workflow Audit cannibalizes the full audit**

Keep it off the homepage. Limit it to one defined workflow. Use it primarily as a downsell and disqualification path. Credit the fee toward a larger engagement. Recommend the full audit when the problem affects multiple departments.

### **There is no audit proof at launch**

Lead with operator credibility rather than unrelated marketing metrics. Publish the sample deliverable. Offer the findings guarantee. Convert the first three audits into case studies as a condition of the launch-period pricing where the client agrees.
